# Continuity and motion

## Continuity lock card

Create this card before per-shot prompts.

### Character lock

- fictional adult identity or authorized reference
- count and relationship
- height/build silhouette
- hair silhouette
- outfit colors, layers, sleeves, belt, footwear
- carried objects
- starting emotional state and permitted change

### Landmark lock

- silhouette and orientation
- roof/floor count
- gate, ring, bridge, stair, waterfall, or tree shape
- dominant materials and motifs
- location relative to character and horizon
- features allowed to remain off-frame

### World lock

- map: start, path, destination
- horizon height and cardinal light direction
- cloud/river/wind direction
- time of day and weather
- palette, neutral anchors, and high-chroma accents
- material family and atmospheric density

## Per-shot motion contract

Specify five items:

1. **Initial state**: stable opening composition.
2. **Subject action**: one readable action chain.
3. **Primary camera move**: type, direction, speed, and approximate extent.
4. **Environmental motion**: one or two subtle layers.
5. **End state**: stable last-frame composition and new information.

Example:

```text
Initial state: a giant pine trunk frames the left edge; one adult traveler stands on the lower-right cliff.
Subject action: the traveler raises their gaze toward the distant gate; sleeves lift once in the wind.
Primary camera move: slow lateral dolly right, revealing the gate through the pine branches; no zoom or roll.
Environmental motion: cloud sea drifts left to right; pine needles move gently.
End state: the gate occupies the right third, the traveler remains 4% of frame height, and the travel path becomes visible.
```

## Camera movement library

- **Slow push-in**: approach emotion, destination, or detail; avoid using in every shot.
- **Pullback reveal**: expand from person/detail to world; strong for opening or scale escalation.
- **Lateral parallax**: reveal hidden architecture behind tree, column, cliff, or doorway.
- **Crane rise/fall**: disclose vertical geography, stairs, waterfall, or city layers.
- **Tracking follow**: clarify traversal while preserving character scale and direction.
- **Tilt reveal**: move from known-size foreground to towering landmark; keep speed slow.
- **Restrained orbit**: use around one stable subject only when geometry can remain coherent.
- **Locked-off tableau**: let cloth, fog, water, foliage, and people provide motion.

## Motion limits

- One primary camera movement per shot.
- Make the stated movement distance and lens capable of producing the described end framing; reduce the framing change or enlarge the move when they conflict.
- One simple subject action chain; do not combine running, turning, drawing a sword, flying, and transforming.
- One or two environmental motion layers.
- No random zoom pulses, camera roll, handheld shake, speed ramp, or rapid orbit unless explicitly requested.
- Keep movement physically continuous from first to last frame.

## Cross-shot continuity

- Preserve travel direction across cuts unless a deliberate axis change is established.
- Preserve landmark screen direction or use an establishing shot before reversing it.
- Match wind direction, fabric response, cloud drift, and light direction.
- Track wetness, damage, opened doors, lifted objects, and character position.
- Do not reset the character to an earlier location without a transition.

## Transitions

Prefer motivated cuts:

- match direction of movement
- cut on gaze toward the next space
- foreground wipe by column, tree, sleeve, cloud, or eave
- match shape between moon gate, celestial ring, sun, or reflection
- sound-motivated cut when audio is part of the plan

Avoid using dissolve between every shot. Use a dissolve only for elapsed time, dream transition, or atmospheric continuity.

## Reference-image roles

Label every image explicitly:

- character identity anchor
- outfit anchor
- landmark anchor
- world/style reference
- strict first frame
- strict last frame
- motion reference

Do not ask one image to be both a strict first frame and a loose style reference without stating priority.
